A new requirement would be MAJOR under this repository's own policy. There is none, and the count is
the proof rather than the intention.

## One deliberate inclusion, declared

`artifacts/review/review-method.md` was authored on the framework branch and is cherry-picked into
the release lineage. It is neither framework code nor a normative change: it records the standing
rule for future audits, written because N13 and N17 were the same reviewer error twice. It changes
no rule contract, no acceptance state, and no behaviour — the contract snapshot and the acceptance
lock are both clean with it present. Including it keeps the evidence and the artefact it describes
on one lineage; noting it here is preferable to letting a reader discover an unexplained file in the
release diff.

## What was deliberately left behind

`v1.3-candidate` at `c6ba9d4` is preserved unmerged. It is the version of this work built against
`v1.2.0`, and it is the evidence that the acceptance hole existed — the commit whose own replay
report says the integrity check could not see N10. Superseding it in place would have destroyed the
record of why `1.3.0` was needed.

## Release claim

> **v1.4.0 introduces seven review-supported normative changes after replay through the
> acceptance-lock invariant. The acceptance lock protects observable semantics for the ten
> machine-examined rules; it does not establish semantic integrity for the remaining thirty-five
> human-judgement rules, or completeness of the normative corpus.**

The 10-of-45 figure is an assurance boundary. It is not the next item of work, and manufacturing
acceptance fixtures for rules that have no mechanically observable predicate would produce the
appearance of protection and none of it.
