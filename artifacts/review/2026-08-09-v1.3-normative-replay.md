## Clarifications: semantic-delta

The test each clarification had to pass:

> Which project was compliant before and is not now? Which was non-compliant before and is now?
> For a clarification, both answers must be none.

Three mechanical assertions, in `test/v13-replay.test.mjs`:

1. **No rule that existed in `v1.2.0` changed its machine-readable contract.** Every rule's `kind`,
   `level`, `severity`, `verification`, `assurance`, `nonExemptible`, `attestable`, `standard`,
   `requirement`, `triggers` and `evidenceExpected` are snapshotted from the `v1.2.0` tag into
   `test/fixtures/v12-rule-contracts.json` and asserted unchanged. Fifty contracts, zero drift.
2. **The set of machine-examined rules is unchanged** — the same ten. A clarification that widened
   automation would change which projects get a verdict rather than which comply, and that is a
   different authorisation.
3. **No rule belonging to a touched standard became machine-evaluated.**

`reproducibility.dependencies-pinned`'s `evidenceExpected` already read *"A requirements file with
exact pins, or a manifest accompanied by a committed lockfile"* in `v1.2.0`. The catalog was already
right; the standard's prose and the detector were the parts out of step. That is why even the one
authorised change produces no contract delta — it is a correction of the text and the mechanism
toward the evidence requirement that was already written.

## Two withdrawals

**N6 failed the semantic-delta test and was correct to fail it.** Extending Standard 20 R3 to cover
a difference lying inside its measured uncertainty makes non-compliant a project that was compliant:
one that quantified its uncertainty and called an inside-the-interval difference "practically
meaningful". That is an added obligation. The disposition classified it as a clarification and the
disposition was wrong — it re-enters as a candidate change, MAJOR-shaped, and is not in this release.

**N17 was rejected on re-reading.** Standard 1 R4's paragraph already says *"Qualitative is
sufficient where quantitative is unavailable"* and gives a worked example. The finding came from
reading the bold normative sentence without the prose beneath it — the same error that produced N13,
which died the same way in disposition.

**Two of eighteen findings were artefacts of reading normative sentences in isolation.** That is a
method defect in the review, not a defect in the corpus, and it is recorded here so a future review
extracts claims with their prose.

## The addition, and where its evidence stops

`evaluation.resampling-not-by-default` is a **recommendation**, at `warning` severity, verified by
`manual-review` with `assurance: none`. It could not be a requirement in this release — that would
be MAJOR — and it should not be a prohibition in any release, because the evidence has a boundary
and the standard names it: weak learners, loss reweighting in deep learning, and extreme rare-event
regimes are all outside what van den Goorbergh (2022) and Elor & Averbuch-Elor (2022) establish.

A test asserts the boundary is present in the standard's text and that the section says `SHOULD NOT`
rather than `MUST NOT`.

## Placement: N3 did not earn a Standard 26

Determined from subject and applicability, as instructed, rather than from the index.

Construct validity — whether the label faithfully measures what the named decision needs — is not
distinct from problem formulation. Standard 1 R1 states what is predicted; Standard 1 R3 names the
decision the output feeds. **The obligation is the join between two claims that already exist in
Standard 1**, and it attaches exactly where they differ: where the predicted quantity is a proxy for
the quantity the decision needs. Its natural home is a Standard 1 R6, not a new standard.

A new standard would have made the index look more comprehensive and would have separated the
obligation from the two claims that define its trigger.

**One part of N3 does not fit there and is not carried by this conclusion.** Label noise and
annotation quality are properties of the data, not of the formulation, and the disposition did not
support them — it supported the proxy-relationship obligation only. They remain unaddressed by the
corpus, and this replay does not quietly fold them into a placement decision that was made about
something else.

N5 and N8 are both honesty-shaped claims about what an estimate supports. Placement is not settled
here: N5 has candidate homes in Standard 5 and Standard 25, N8 in Standard 8 and in a new standard,
and neither was analysed to the depth N3 was because neither can enter a MINOR release.

## The integrity mechanism, and what it did not catch

The candidate was built with `scripts/integrity.mjs` live, unmodified, and not special-cased.

**It fired once, correctly, and in the right register.** Adding the recommendation produced:

```text
  Locked rules: 50    Live rules: 51
  Not yet in the baseline (new rules are not violations, but the baseline should cover them):
    + evaluation.resampling-not-by-default
```

Followed by a deliberate relock — the new entry added to `artifacts/catalog-baseline.json`, the
framework version stamped to `1.3.0`, and the reason recorded in `CHANGELOG.md`. The authorised
transition ran end to end:

```text
frozen baseline → review finding → disposition evidence → candidate change
    → integrity reports the divergence → deliberate relock with justification → valid
```

Two tests also failed at the moment the rule was added, and both were the machinery working: the
integrity test asserting the unlocked set, and the dogfood policy test asserting that this
repository declares an applicability for **every** rule in the catalog. Adding a rule obliges the
repository to say what it does about it, which is the dogfooding requirement doing its job on the
release that changes the catalog.

### And a limitation it exposed

**INV-1 did not fire for N10, and it could not have.** The baseline locks `kind`, `level`,
`severity`, `verification`, `assurance`, `nonExemptible` and `attestable`. It does not lock what
evidence satisfies a rule — so widening `reproducibility.dependencies-pinned` from *pins only* to
*pins or a lock artifact* is invisible to the integrity check, even though it is a real loosening of
what a project must produce.

That is a gap in the lock, discovered by trying to use it. It is **recorded and not fixed**: closing
it is framework work, no supported disposition authorises it, and this candidate is confined to
changes traceable to `80a5a82`. It belongs on the register for whatever comes after.
