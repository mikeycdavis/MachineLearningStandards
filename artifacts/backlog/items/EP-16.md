## Outcome

**Every gate that reads a document by a convention of its own now states it, and departing from it
fails loudly.** Both halves of the `What` are satisfied, and the second was measured rather than
asserted.

`design/architecture.md` gained two sections at the authoring surface. §13 states the
normative-sentence convention `levels` reads, delivered by [[FE-35]]; §14 states the claim/block
contract `fidelity` reads, delivered by [[FE-36]]. Each was paired with a guard change, because a
convention written down but unenforced is still silently violable and enforcement without a written
rule fails an author for a rule they cannot read.

Each gate's silent shape is closed. `levels` reported agreement from whichever bold span came
first; more than one modal-bearing span is now `ambiguous-normative-sentence`. `fidelity` passed a
claim whose following material it could not resolve; that is now `no-block`. Both were measured
against the shipped checkers before repair, and both are held by mutations rather than by a green
run over a green corpus.

**The survey this epic named is complete, and two gates needed nothing.** `diagrams.mjs` binds to
ordinary Markdown ` ```mermaid ` fences rather than a private convention and compares each source
against *every* embedded block, having already been bitten by the pairing version and fixed with a
mutation test. `inventory.mjs` reads the spec by regex, but the committed human-reviewed lock turns
any extraction shortfall into a missing item rather than into silence — probed by removing the dot
from one item heading, which produced `Missing source numbers: 11` and exit 1. Neither is filed,
and neither is an omission.

**One limit, which is not unfinished work.** Tightening claim recognition narrowed it: a derivation
verb with no reproduction word, and a claim ended with a full stop, are no longer recognised. That
is a deliberate trade recorded in §14 — the alternative admits any `<verb> from the source:`,
including sentences introducing a list of differences — and §14 states the accepted forms so an
author can comply. If a corpus ever needs a form outside them, that is a new item, not a reopening
of this one.

## What

Where a gate reads a document by convention rather than by contract, state the convention where an
author will find it, and make a document that departs from it fail loudly rather than be misread.

## Why

[[FE-33]] added `scripts/levels.mjs`, which decides a rule's stated level by reading the first bold
span in its section. That reading is correct for all twenty-five standards as written, and it was
never written down anywhere. A convention nobody recorded is one an author can violate without
knowing it existed.

That would be a documentation problem on its own. It is more than one because the misreading is
silent in one shape: a section whose bold lead carries a modal is read as the rule's level even
when the real normative sentence beneath says something weaker. The gate then reports agreement for
a rule whose prose and catalog genuinely disagree — the exact defect it was built to catch.

This sits under IN-04 because IN-04 is the initiative that owns how far a guard's protection
actually reaches: the acceptance lock was added there when the integrity check was found to lock a
rule's classification but not its satisfaction semantics. This is the same shape one level further
out — a guard whose reach depends on an assumption nobody stated.

## Notes

**The outcome is met for `levels` and not yet for the epic.** [[FE-35]] closed on 2026-08-28:
`design/architecture.md` §13 states the normative-sentence convention at the authoring surface, and
a section with more than one modal-bearing bold span is now a finding rather than a tie broken
silently. That discharges this epic's `What` for one gate.

**The look this epic named has now been done, and it found a second one.** `diagrams.mjs` is clear:
it binds to ordinary Markdown ```` ```mermaid ```` fences rather than a private convention, and it
compares each `.mmd` against *every* embedded block rather than pairing one, having already been
bitten by the pairing version and fixed with a mutation test.

`fidelity.mjs` is not clear, and this was measured rather than reasoned about. It reads a claim line
matching `CLAIM_RE` and checks the block immediately after it — a fence, a blockquote, or a list.
Where the following material is none of those, `blockAfter` returns null and the claim is passed
over in silence. A probe section reading `Reproduced verbatim from the source:` above a plain
paragraph whose text appears nowhere in the spec left the run at **25 claims checked, 0 unverified,
exit 0**. That is a verbatim claim asserted and never tested, and neither half of the convention —
that the claim sentence is what triggers the check, or that it must be followed immediately by a
fence, quotation or list — is stated anywhere an author would find it.

That is this epic's outcome, unmet, for a second gate, and it is now filed as [[FE-36]] — state
what must follow a verbatim-source claim, and stop a claim with nothing to check passing. The
scoping question that held it back has been answered by measurement: **zero** of the 25 live claims
lack a supported block, so the rule needs no allowlist and no exemption, and what implementation
must confront instead is that the corpus exercises only one of the three accepted forms and that
`CLAIM_RE` is loose in a way the skip currently masks.

**This epic is now fully planned, and is not therefore complete.** Both gates that read a document
by a private convention are accounted for — `levels` by [[FE-35]], closed, and `fidelity` by
[[FE-36]], open — and `diagrams.mjs` was measured as binding to ordinary Markdown rather than to a
convention of its own. "Fully planned" is a statement about what has been identified, not a claim
of completeness: this epic closes on its outcome, the way [[EP-15]] did, and its outcome is unmet
while a verbatim claim can still be asserted and never tested.

**Why not an existing epic.** EP-03 owns the twenty-five normative documents and is the natural
home for how they are written — but it is COMPLETE, under a COMPLETE initiative, and reopening it
to hang a later question on would falsify a closure that was correct when it was made. That is the
reasoning [[EP-15]] recorded when it declined to reopen EP-04 for the same kind of reason.

**Why not [[EP-15]].** EP-15 is COMPLETE and its outcome is met: twenty-six of twenty-six entries
agree with the sections they name, verified. Its `What` is about deciding which representation
governs and getting the corpus to agree, both of which happened. This is about a guard's reach,
not about a disagreement — no evidence says EP-15's outcome is unmet, so it stays closed.
