## What

Execute the repair [ADR 0012](../../adr/0012-three-prose-modals-are-wrong-and-two-catalog-levels-are.md)
decided, so that a reader of a standard's prose and a reader of its catalog entry reach the same
answer about its level.

Two records govern this item and are **not** re-opened by it:

- [**ADR 0011**](../../adr/0011-the-catalog-states-a-rules-level.md) is the **authority rule** —
  *the catalog states a rule's normative level; the prose states the rule*, and authority is not
  correctness.
- [**ADR 0012**](../../adr/0012-three-prose-modals-are-wrong-and-two-catalog-levels-are.md) is the
  **per-rule repair disposition**. Its directions are authoritative evidence for this item. This
  item implements them; it does not re-disposition them, and disagreement with a direction is a
  new decision record, never a quiet variation during execution.

## Why

[[EP-15]]'s outcome is that *"a future reader of either one alone reaches the same answer"*, and
[[FE-32]] established that nothing else satisfies it. Today a reader of Standard 16 R1's prose
alone reads `MUST` for a rule the framework will only warn on, and a reader of Standard 20 R1's
catalog entry alone reads `recommendation` for an obligation the brief states as *"Require"*.

Edit 6 is not tidiness. Standard 20 R3 currently has no entry of its own — its claim survives as a
trailing clause of `evaluation.uncertainty-reported`'s `description`. Raising that entry for edit 5
would therefore **relevel R3 as a side effect of repairing R1**, which is precisely the silent
reclassification `invariant.standards-integrity` exists to prevent. Splitting R3 out is what makes
edit 5 safe, which is also why the six cannot be applied in arbitrary order.

## Release class

**The aggregate repair is MAJOR.** Edits 4 and 5 each satisfy two clauses of the versioning policy
independently — *"adding a requirement"* and *"raising a rule's level"* — so the combined vehicle
takes the class of its largest part. The three softenings are MINOR individually and edit 6 is a
recommendation addition, but ADR 0012 established the six cannot be split across vehicles: edits 5
and 6 touch the same entry, and edit 4 is what makes Standard 19 P2 — a `prohibition` at `error`
severity — enforceable at all. Shipping the softenings alone would weaken three standards while
leaving both incoherences standing.

**MAJOR is a release classification, not release authorization.** Filing this item creates no
release, no version bump, no `2.0.0` vehicle and no tag, and nothing here authorizes one. That
follows the standing boundary [ADR 0009](../../adr/0009-fairness-is-conditionally-in-scope.md),
ADR 0011 and ADR 0012 all closed under, and it matches how this backlog has already treated
MAJOR-class work: [[FE-24]] has held three requirement additions since 2026-08-09 with no paired
release item, and [[EP-12]] records that *"no drafting has been authorised."* No backlog policy
requires a release item at filing time, and none is filed.

One qualification worth stating plainly: MAJOR here does **not** mean the corpus asks more than it
does today. Edits 4 and 5 make the catalog carry an obligation the prose already states and the
brief already required. What genuinely changes is the contract with a consuming project — a warning
becomes an error — and that is what the class is measuring.

## Notes

**Why EP-15 and not EP-12, checked rather than assumed.** [[EP-12]] has a real claim: it is the
epic for normative work a MINOR release cannot carry, and edits 4 and 5 are exactly that shape.
Three things decide it against EP-12.

- **Lineage.** Every one of EP-12's four children — [[FE-24]], [[FE-25]], [[FE-26]], [[FE-30]] — is
  a finding from the 2026-08-09 normative review. This repair is not; it descends from the
  authority investigation [[FE-31]] and [[FE-32]], both of which are EP-15's.
- **Outcome delivered.** EP-15's outcome is unmet *because* this repair has not happened, and no
  other item can satisfy it. Parenting it under EP-12 would leave EP-15 permanently uncompletable
  by its own children, which breaks the roll-up the hierarchy exists to give.
- **Addition versus reconciliation.** EP-12 holds obligations that exist nowhere in the corpus
  today. This item adds no obligation a reader is not already given: 19 R3 and 20 R1 already say
  `MUST` in prose. It moves where an obligation is recorded, and corrects three modals the source
  never supported.

The one sentence that reads against this placement is EP-15's own — *"this question is upstream of
[EP-12] and produces no normative content"*. That was written about the authority **question**, and
EP-15's original Notes anticipated this item explicitly: *"if the catalog governs, the prose in the
affected standards is what moves."* The epic pre-registered the work; it did not exclude it.

**Why one feature and not six.** Tested against the three reasons that would justify splitting, and
none holds. There is no ownership boundary — all six edits are the same person's, in the same two
directories. There is no independent shippability: ADR 0012 established the six travel in one
vehicle. And the sequencing constraint that does exist — edit 6 must land with or before edit 5 —
argues *for* one item, because splitting them creates a reachable intermediate state in which R3
has been relevelled by accident, which is the exact defect the repair exists to avoid. Six
per-rule items would also restate one shared target state and one shared release consequence six
times.

Decomposition into stories was considered and rejected on the hierarchy's own test: a story is a
user-visible change of roughly one pull request, and none of the six can ship alone. The six edits
are recorded as the completion condition instead of as child items.

**A guard will fail, and that is the point.** `test/v13-replay.test.mjs` asserts that the only
catalog addition since the `v13-rule-contracts.json` fixture is `evaluation.resampling-not-by-default`
and that its kind is `recommendation`. Edit 6 adds a second entry, so that assertion fails until it
is updated. It was written so *"a later edit cannot slip one in under a MINOR release"* — so the
failure is the guard working, and updating it is part of this item's deliberate act, not an
obstacle to route around. `artifacts/acceptance-baseline.json` should be checked for the same
reason before the work is called done.

**N6 is not reopened by filing this.** [[FE-25]] stays `COMPLETE` with N6 dispositioned
`SUPPORTED_FOR_CHANGE` and classified MINOR at the level Standard 20 R3 is carried at today.
Nothing here revisits that.

**N6 touches the same surface, and its sequencing is deliberately left open.** N6 modifies Standard
20 R3's claim, and edits 5 and 6 restructure the entry that carries it — so the two will meet. How
they meet is a planning decision to be taken when this work is actually scheduled, with N6's class
re-derived against the catalog as it stands at that moment. It is **not** silently folded into this
item: N6 is not in the completion condition above, and this item can be executed without it.

**[[FE-33]] stays separate and is not started by this.** It is framework work — a mechanical check
— not corpus repair. What it gains from ADR 0012 is one assumption it could not previously make:
the intended steady state has **zero** allowed mismatches, so the check needs no allowlist of
permitted exceptions. That is a design fact, not a trigger. FE-33 must not be started merely
because this item now exists, and it still cannot gate CI until this item lands.

**`NOT_STARTED` means not scheduled, not un-actionable.** Every input the work needs is recorded.
What it lacks is a vehicle, and a vehicle is not this item's to create.
