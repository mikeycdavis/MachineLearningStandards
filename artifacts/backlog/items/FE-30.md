## What

Decide whether disparate performance across protected or vulnerable groups belongs within the
normative scope of this corpus, and record the decision with its rationale.

This item is **decision-shaped, not implementation-shaped**. It completes when the scope boundary
is decided and written down. It does not require adding fairness standards.

- If the decision is **outside**, this completes with no normative additions, and the absence
  becomes an accepted exclusion rather than an unexamined one.
- If the decision is **inside**, this still completes on the recorded decision. Any drafting that
  follows is separately justified normative work under its own items — the scope decision does not
  silently become a drafting task.

## Why

N4's disposition is `DEFERRED`, and the record is explicit that a scope decision about the
corpus's subject must precede any drafting. The unresolved question is not whether a fairness
requirement would be well drafted; it is whether this corpus is the thing that carries one.

Until that is decided, the absence of protected-group standards is **neither an accepted exclusion
nor an identified gap** — it is undetermined. The disposition record states that the line was drawn
implicitly by what the source prompt happened to enumerate, and that it "has never been made
explicitly."

Left only inside the disposition record, an unresolved decision about this system's own normative
boundary is too easy to lose.

## Notes

The counterexample the review already established constrains any "inside" answer, and survives
into whatever is drafted: a universal `MUST` is unsatisfiable where the attributes are not
collected, irrelevant where there are no people, and in some cases a privacy harm to satisfy —
collecting a protected attribute in order to measure fairness against it is a real cost.
Unsatisfiable requirements get waived, which teaches a project that waiving is normal.

Note also that hiding a known disparity is already prohibited by Standard 25 P1. What is
undetermined is whether the corpus requires the disparity to be *constructed and looked for*, not
whether it may be concealed.

The `evidence` entry points at the disposition record that establishes the `DEFERRED` state and
the unresolved boundary — not at an implementation plan, which does not exist and should not be
invented to give this item a shape it has not earned.

Distinct from [[FE-26]], which is an epistemic question about completeness, and from N18
(multiplicity), which stays out of the backlog on purpose: `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` preserves a
known problem whose remedy is not established, and filing it would convert "do not rediscover
this" into "solve this." Evidence establishing an actionable mechanism is its trigger.
