## Outcome

**The catalog states a rule's normative level; the prose states the rule.** Recorded in
[ADR 0011](../../adr/0011-the-catalog-states-a-rules-level.md). Where the two disagree about
level, the catalog governs; where they disagree about anything else, the prose governs. Authority
is not correctness — the rule settles which text states the level, not whether that level is right.

**Standard 20 R3's present level is `recommended`.** R3 has no catalog entry of its own; its claim
is carried inside `evaluation.uncertainty-reported`, which is bound to R1 and is a recommendation.

**The corpus is internally inconsistent in five places**, measured corpus-wide rather than inferred
from Standard 20: five of the seven catalog recommendations have `MUST` prose — Standards 11 R2,
16 R1, 18 R2, 19 R3 and 20 R1. Forty-one of forty-six entries agree, so this is an outlier class,
not a convention. The eighty-nine prose sections with no catalog entry are not defects; the catalog
has never been a one-to-one projection of the prose.

**N6 is MINOR.** All four MAJOR clauses were tested against the minimal faithful implementation and
all four fail.

The decision was reached by rejecting prose-authority on a mechanical ground — `integrity.mjs`
would protect a copy, since it never opens `standards/` — and by falsifying the authority-by-concern
alternative on measurement: twelve `requirement` entries carry the same `manual-review` /
`assurance: none` posture as the five prose-`MUST` recommendations, so no principled boundary
separates them.

## What

A decision, recorded the way FE-30's was, settling which of two representations governs when a
standard's prose and its catalog entry state a rule's level differently — and applying it to the
case that raised the question, Standard 20 R3.

The two representations, as they stand today:

| | Says |
| --- | --- |
| `standards/20-uncertainty.md` R3 | **Where uncertainty has not been quantified, a small difference MUST NOT be described as an improvement** |
| `rules/evaluation.json` → `evaluation.uncertainty-reported` | `kind: recommendation`, `level: recommended`, `severity: warning`, bound to **R1**; R3 survives only as a trailing clause of its `description` |
| `design/architecture.md` | maps `kind: recommendation` to *"should"* and *"should normally"* |

## Why

Semantic versioning in this repository turns on *"raising a rule's level"*, and a level stated two
ways cannot decide a release class. N6 is `SUPPORTED_FOR_CHANGE` and its release class is
undetermined solely because of this — MINOR at the level R3 is carried at, MAJOR at the level R3's
prose claims.

It also bears on `invariant.standards-integrity`. The integrity guard locks the catalog's
`{kind, level, nonExemptible}` against a committed baseline. If prose is the authority, that guard
protects a copy rather than the original, and a corpus could be weakened in the prose without the
baseline moving.

## Completion condition

A recorded decision — an ADR is the fitting form, following
[ADR 0009](../../adr/0009-fairness-is-conditionally-in-scope.md) — establishing all four of:

1. **Which representation governs** when standard prose and catalog level disagree, stated as a
   general rule rather than as a ruling about one standard.
2. **Whether Standard 20 R3 is presently a requirement or a recommendation**, under that rule.
3. **The evidence and rationale** for the decision, including whichever of the two the corpus was
   actually built to treat as authoritative — the three-way separation in
   [ADR 0001](../../adr/0001-standalone-domain-first-design.md) and
   `design/architecture.md`'s concept table are both operative text here, and so is the fact that
   Standard 20 appears in none of the ten evaluated rules.
4. **The consequence for N6's release class** — which of MINOR or MAJOR follows, stated as an
   implication of the decision and not as a release plan.

## Notes

**Decision-shaped, not implementation-shaped.** It is complete when the decision is recorded. It
does not edit Standard 20, does not edit the catalog, does not touch either baseline, and schedules
no release. A class is evidence about a future vehicle, never authorisation to cut one — the
boundary FE-30 closed under, and the one
[the N6 re-disposition](../../review/2026-08-26-n6-redisposition.md) stops at.

**Actionable now, unscheduled.** `NOT_STARTED` here is "not scheduled", not "not actionable": every
text the decision needs is already in this repository, and nothing blocks starting. It is left
unstarted deliberately.

**What raised it, and what it does not disturb.** It came out of FE-25, which is COMPLETE at
`5a2cb4f` with N6 dispositioned `SUPPORTED_FOR_CHANGE`. FE-25 is not reopened by this item and the
disposition is not revisited. FE-24 remains independently MAJOR-gated — adding a requirement is
MAJOR verbatim, with no dependence on this question — and FE-26 remains non-executable pending an
independent review.

**The disagreement is pre-existing, not introduced.** Standard 20 has stated its rules as `MUST`
while carrying one `recommendation` since `1.0.0`. Nothing in N6 created it; N6 only made it
load-bearing.
