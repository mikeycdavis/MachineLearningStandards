## Outcome

**`SUPPORTED_FOR_CHANGE`.** Recorded in
[the re-disposition](../../review/2026-08-26-n6-redisposition.md). The remedy modifies an existing
claim, so `SUPPORTED_FOR_CLARIFICATION` was the wrong class and the replay was right to withdraw
it: extending 20 R3 to a difference lying inside its measured uncertainty makes non-compliant a
project that quantified its uncertainty and called such a difference practically meaningful. The
sample-size remedy stays rejected on the original counterexample.

The **release** class is a separate axis and is deliberately left open. Standard 20 R3 has no
catalog entry of its own — the standard is carried by one `recommendation`, `evaluation.uncertainty-reported`,
bound to R1 — so the delta is MINOR at the level R3 is carried at today and MAJOR at the level R3's
prose claims. Which one governs turns on a pre-existing disagreement between Standard 20's `MUST`
prose and its catalog level, which is recorded in the same document and is not this item's question.

## What

Extending Standard 20 R3 to differences inside their measured uncertainty.

## Why

It was withdrawn from the 1.4.0 candidate mid-replay: it adds an obligation, so it is a
normative change rather than a clarification, and it cannot travel as one.

## Notes

Withdrawn rather than rejected. The finding stands; only its classification was wrong.

This item closed on the disposition, not on a normative edit. Standard 20, the catalog, both
baselines, the acceptance fixtures, `VERSION`, `package.json` and `CHANGELOG.md` are all unchanged,
and no release was scheduled. A class is evidence about a future vehicle, not authorisation to cut
one — the same boundary FE-30 closed under.
