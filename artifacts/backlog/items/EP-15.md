## What

Where a standard's prose and its catalog entry state a rule's level differently, decide which
representation governs, and record the decision so that a future reader of either one alone reaches
the same answer.

## Why

`invariant.standards-integrity` forbids reclassifying a rule to reach a desired conclusion, and
`scripts/integrity.mjs` enforces that against `artifacts/catalog-baseline.json` — which locks
`{kind, level, nonExemptible}` as the catalog states them. That guard is only as strong as the
claim that the catalog is where a rule's level lives. Nothing checks the catalog's level against
the `MUST` / `SHOULD` the standard's own prose uses, and
[the N6 re-disposition](../../review/2026-08-26-n6-redisposition.md) found a case where the two
disagree.

The consequence is not hypothetical. Semantic versioning here turns on *"raising a rule's level"*,
so a release class cannot be established while a rule has two levels. N6's class is undetermined
today for exactly that reason.

This sits under IN-04 rather than EP-12. EP-12 holds supported normative content waiting for a
release vehicle; this question is upstream of that and produces no normative content — it decides
what a rule's classification *is*, which is the half of IN-04's title that the acceptance lock
deliberately did not address.

## Notes

**One identified child today, and no programme behind it.** Whether this epic holds anything beyond
FE-31 depends on FE-31's outcome: if the prose governs, some catalog entries may be misclassified
and a corpus-wide audit becomes real work; if the catalog governs, the prose in the affected
standards is what moves; and a mechanical check that the two agree may be worth building under
either. **None of that is filed**, because none of it is established. Filing it now would be
inventing scope from a decision that has not been made — the same restraint FE-30 recorded when it
closed on a scope decision without scheduling the work that decision implied.

The epic exists because the hierarchy requires a feature's parent to be exactly one level up, and
no existing epic owns this outcome. EP-04 arguably does — *"the catalog defines identity and
metadata"* — but it is complete, under a complete initiative, and reopening it to hang a later
question on would falsify a closure that was correct when made.

**Why this did not close with FE-31.** `IN_PROGRESS` here is parent/child state coherence and an
unmet outcome, not a start on further work. FE-31 completed the half of this epic's `What` that
asks which representation governs; ADR 0011 answers it. The other half — *"so that a future reader
of either one alone reaches the same answer"* — is not satisfied: a reader of Standard 20's prose
alone still reads `MUST` and concludes `required`, and the same holds for Standards 11 R2, 16 R1,
18 R2 and 19 R3. Five entries disagree with their prose, and ADR 0011 named them without repairing
them, because repair is a normative act with a direction to choose per rule.

So this epic is legitimately open over work nobody has written down, which is the case
`backlog-reconcile` warns will look like a forgotten parent. It is not forgotten. Whether the five
repairs, or a mechanical prose-to-catalog check, become items is `backlog`'s decision and has not
been taken.
