## Outcome

**Both halves of the `What` are satisfied, and the second was verified rather than asserted.**

Which representation governs was decided by [[FE-31]] — the catalog states a rule's level, the
prose states the rule (ADR 0011). That a reader of either one alone now reaches the same answer was
delivered by [[FE-32]] choosing a repair direction per rule (ADR 0012) and [[FE-34]] executing all
six edits: **26 of 26 requirement and recommendation entries agree with the sections they name,
zero mismatches**. [[FE-33]] made that state durable — `scripts/levels.mjs` is stage 5 of the CI
pipeline, so the link that let the last defect through is now watched (ADR 0013).

The `Why` is discharged with it. `invariant.standards-integrity` guards the catalog's levels, and
the claim that the catalog is where a level lives is no longer only a claim — it is decided,
repaired into agreement, and mechanically held. Release class was the concrete consequence named
here, and N6's is now determined rather than blocked on a rule with two levels.

**One stated limit, which is not unfinished work.** Prohibitions are not level-checked: their
sections quote a must-never bullet rather than stating a modal, so no level convention exists for
them to be checked against, and inventing one inside a gate was refused. That is an assurance
boundary of the kind [[FE-26]] distinguishes from a gap, recorded in ADR 0013 rather than filed. If
a convention is ever established, the work it implies is a new item and not a reopening of this
one.

## What

Where a standard's prose and its catalog entry state a rule's level differently, decide which
representation governs, and record the decision so that a future reader of either one alone reaches
the same answer.

## Why

`invariant.standards-integrity` forbids reclassifying a rule to reach a desired conclusion, and
`scripts/integrity.mjs` enforces that against `artifacts/catalog-baseline.json` — which locks
`{kind, level, nonExemptible}` as the catalog states them. That guard is only as strong as the
claim that the catalog is where a rule's level lives. Nothing checks the catalog's level against
the `MUST` / `SHOULD` the standard's own prose uses, and
[the N6 re-disposition](../../review/2026-08-26-n6-redisposition.md) found a case where the two
disagree.

The consequence is not hypothetical. Semantic versioning here turns on *"raising a rule's level"*,
so a release class cannot be established while a rule has two levels. N6's class is undetermined
today for exactly that reason.

This sits under IN-04 rather than EP-12. EP-12 holds supported normative content waiting for a
release vehicle; this question is upstream of that and produces no normative content — it decides
what a rule's classification *is*, which is the half of IN-04's title that the acceptance lock
deliberately did not address.

## Notes

**One identified child today, and no programme behind it.** Whether this epic holds anything beyond
FE-31 depends on FE-31's outcome: if the prose governs, some catalog entries may be misclassified
and a corpus-wide audit becomes real work; if the catalog governs, the prose in the affected
standards is what moves; and a mechanical check that the two agree may be worth building under
either. **None of that is filed**, because none of it is established. Filing it now would be
inventing scope from a decision that has not been made — the same restraint FE-30 recorded when it
closed on a scope decision without scheduling the work that decision implied.

The epic exists because the hierarchy requires a feature's parent to be exactly one level up, and
no existing epic owns this outcome. EP-04 arguably does — *"the catalog defines identity and
metadata"* — but it is complete, under a complete initiative, and reopening it to hang a later
question on would falsify a closure that was correct when made.

**Why this did not close with FE-31.** `IN_PROGRESS` here is parent/child state coherence and an
unmet outcome, not a start on further work. FE-31 completed the half of this epic's `What` that
asks which representation governs; ADR 0011 answers it. The other half — *"so that a future reader
of either one alone reaches the same answer"* — is not satisfied: a reader of Standard 20's prose
alone still reads `MUST` and concludes `required`, and the same holds for Standards 11 R2, 16 R1,
18 R2 and 19 R3. Five entries disagree with their prose, and ADR 0011 named them without repairing
them, because repair is a normative act with a direction to choose per rule.

**That decision has now been taken.** On 2026-08-26 the two remaining questions were filed as
[[FE-32]] — the repair direction for each of the five, which ADR 0011 deliberately did not choose —
and [[FE-33]] — a mechanical check that a catalog entry and its standard agree, closing the one
provenance link with no guard. Both were filed because the evidence supports them, not because a
parent with all children closed looks untidy: the epic's `What` asks that *"a future reader of
either one alone reaches the same answer"*, and five readers of prose alone still reach the wrong
one. This epic is `IN_PROGRESS` over identified, unscheduled work, which is what that status is
for.

**FE-32 closed the same day**, on [ADR 0012](../../adr/0012-three-prose-modals-are-wrong-and-two-catalog-levels-are.md):
three prose modals soften, two catalog levels rise, and the combined repair vehicle is MAJOR. That
decided the direction and deliberately did not perform the repair — so a reader of Standard 16's
prose alone still reads `MUST` for a rule the framework will only warn on, and this epic's outcome
is still unmet. Executing the repair was filed the same day as [[FE-34]], once the direction was
decided and the work was well-defined enough to describe without inventing it. [[FE-33]] remains
open and cannot gate until those repairs land.

**This epic's planned work is now fully filed**, and both remaining children are open: FE-34 (the
corpus repair, MAJOR-class and without a vehicle) and FE-33 (the mechanical check, which cannot
gate until FE-34 lands). Nothing further is known to be missing — but "fully filed" is a statement
about what has been identified, not a claim of completeness, and this epic closes on its outcome
rather than on its child count.

A third finding from FE-31 — that `CHANGELOG.md`'s MINOR clause enumerates *"widening what is
accepted"* and never names narrowing — was examined and deliberately **not** filed. It belongs to
release governance rather than to corpus/catalog agreement, and the reasoning is recorded in
[the scope note](../scope.md) so the absence is not read as oversight.
