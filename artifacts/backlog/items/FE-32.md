## Outcome

**Three prose modals are wrong, and two catalog levels are.** Recorded in
[ADR 0012](../../adr/0012-three-prose-modals-are-wrong-and-two-catalog-levels-are.md).

| Standard | Disposition | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 R2 | soften prose `MUST` → `SHOULD` | MINOR |
| 16 R1 | soften prose `MUST` → `SHOULD` | MINOR |
| 18 R2 | soften prose `MUST` → `SHOULD` | MINOR |
| 19 R3 | raise catalog to `requirement` / `required` / `error` | **MAJOR** |
| 20 R1 | raise catalog to `requirement` / `required` / `error` | **MAJOR** |

No rule was dispositioned `insufficient evidence`, and no blanket direction was used. What
separated them is a provenance asymmetry [ADR 0002](../../adr/0002-the-spec-is-a-derived-enumeration.md)
already established: the brief's `## Evaluation philosophy` is byte-identical source and says
*"Require"*, while each numbered item's normative sentences are authored derivation and say
*"should"*. 19 R3 and 20 R1 descend from the philosophy and quote it under `fidelity.mjs`; the
other three descend only from authored item prose, and the brief's Cover list assigns them no level
at all.

Three further findings supported the split independently: `reproducibility.seeds-recorded` is the
only entry of the five whose `rationale` states its level as a reasoned choice, and the strength it
declines is already carried at `required` by `reproducibility.experiment-config-recorded`; the
unsatisfiability counterexample that defeats raising 11 R2 and 18 R2 does not reach 20 R1, because
*where appropriate* governs the form and not whether; and Standard 19 P2 is a `prohibition` at
`error` severity that is unenforceable unless 19 R3 is a requirement.

**Standard 20 R3 gets a catalog entry of its own**, at `recommendation`, with its prose softened to
`SHOULD NOT`. The reason is mechanical rather than tidy: R3 rides inside
`evaluation.uncertainty-reported`'s `description`, so raising that entry would relevel R3 as a side
effect of repairing R1.

**The combined repair vehicle is MAJOR**, and the five cannot be split across vehicles. That is
evidence about a future release and not authorisation to cut one. **N6's class is unchanged** —
still MINOR at the level R3 is carried at today — and [[FE-25]] is not reopened.

## What

For each of the five rules whose standard prose states `MUST` while its catalog entry states
`recommendation`, decide **which side is wrong** — and record the decision with its evidence.

| Standard | Section | Catalog entry | `verification` / `assurance` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | R2 | `evaluation.class-imbalance-addressed` | manual-review / none |
| 16 | R1 | `reproducibility.seeds-recorded` | code-analysis / partial |
| 18 | R2 | `evaluation.ablation-performed` | manual-review / none |
| 19 | R3 | `evaluation.improvement-classification` | manual-review / none |
| 20 | R1 | `evaluation.uncertainty-reported` | manual-review / none |

Two directions are available per rule, and they are not the same size:

- **Soften the prose** to `SHOULD`, accepting that the corpus asks less than it currently reads as
  asking. Changes no rule's level and no project's verdict.
- **Raise the catalog** to `requirement` / `required`, accepting the prose as the correct
  statement. This is *"raising a rule's level"* — MAJOR verbatim — and requires a deliberate
  `artifacts/catalog-baseline.json` update, which is the one route `scripts/integrity.mjs` exists
  to make deliberate rather than quiet.

**The answer is required per rule, not once for all five.** They may resolve differently, and one
of them plainly might: `reproducibility.seeds-recorded` is the only one of the five in
`EVALUATED_RULES`, so raising it changes live verdicts on real target repositories, while the other
four are manual-review with `assurance: none`, where raising the level changes what the framework
*asks* and nothing it mechanically *does*. A single blanket direction would be a decision reached
by counting rather than by evidence.

## Why

[ADR 0011](../../adr/0011-the-catalog-states-a-rules-level.md) settled which representation
*governs*, and stopped exactly one step short of repair, on purpose:

> Repair is a normative act with a direction to choose per rule, and choosing five directions is
> not something a decision about authority is entitled to do implicitly.

The authority rule is **not** an answer to this question. ADR 0011 states as part of the decision
that *"authority is not correctness"* — the catalog is where a reader learns a level, which says
nothing about whether the level found there is right. Treating the catalog as correct **because**
it is authoritative would convert a rule about where to read into a rule about what is true, and
would silently soften five obligations by procedure rather than by decision.

Until this is decided, [[EP-15]]'s stated outcome is measurably unmet: a reader of Standard 16's
prose alone concludes `required`, and the framework will only warn.

## Completion condition

A recorded decision — an ADR, following [ADR 0009](../../adr/0009-fairness-is-conditionally-in-scope.md)
and [ADR 0011](../../adr/0011-the-catalog-states-a-rules-level.md) — establishing all four of:

1. **A direction per rule**, all five named individually, each with the evidence for that
   direction. "All five the same way" is an acceptable outcome only if the evidence reaches it
   separately five times.
2. **What the source spec supports.** These five sections cite `artifacts/prompts/ml-standards-spec.md`
   by item number, and the fidelity chain means the spec's own strength is evidence about which
   side drifted. A prose `MUST` that quotes the source verbatim is a different case from one the
   standard added.
3. **The release class each direction implies**, aggregated — because if any single rule resolves
   as *raise the catalog*, the set contains a MAJOR change and the five would travel together
   rather than separately.
4. **Whether Standard 20 R3 gets a catalog entry of its own**, since it presently has none and its
   claim survives only inside `evaluation.uncertainty-reported`'s `description`. That is a
   representation gap this decision touches and no other item owns.

## Notes

**Decision-shaped, not implementation-shaped.** It completes when the directions are recorded. It
does not edit a standard, the catalog, either baseline, `VERSION`, `package.json`, `CHANGELOG.md`
or the acceptance fixtures, and it schedules no release — the boundary [[FE-30]] and [[FE-31]] both
closed under. A class is evidence about a future vehicle, never authorisation to cut one.

**One item, not five.** The five share one measurement, one authority rule, and one aggregated
release consequence, and any of them resolving upward binds the rest to the same vehicle. Five
files restating that shared context would be the ticket-tracker failure this backlog exists to
avoid. The per-rule answer is enforced by the completion condition instead of by the file count.

**Pre-existing, not introduced.** These disagreements have stood since `1.0.0`. Nothing in N6
created them; [[FE-25]] only made one of them load-bearing.

**Actionable now, unscheduled.** `NOT_STARTED` means not scheduled. Every text the decision needs
— the five standards, the spec, the catalog, ADR 0011 — is already in this repository.

**Does not disturb the settled facts.** [[FE-25]] stays COMPLETE with N6 at `SUPPORTED_FOR_CHANGE`
and MINOR; N6's class was established at the level R3 is carried at *today*, and this item is not a
route to re-open it. [[FE-24]] remains independently MAJOR-gated. [[FE-26]] remains non-executable
pending an independent review.
